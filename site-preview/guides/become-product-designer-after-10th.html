In short

  • Yes, you can start after Class 10. The first year builds UI and UX basics, which every product designer needs first.
  • The difference shows in years 2 and 3: product discovery, roadmaps, metrics, design systems and AI product design.
  • Product designers think like owners. They ask "should we build this?" before "how should it look?"

A product designer owns how a whole product works, not just how one screen looks. They help decide which problem to solve and design the full experience. They keep it consistent with a design system and check with data whether it worked.

Can you start on the path to a product designer right after Class 10?

Yes. You can start working towards product design right after Class 10, because the program begins with UI and UX basics and assumes no design background, then moves to whole-product thinking in year 2.

Product design mixes design, business and technology, so more than one stream works. Each choice tilts your strengths in a different direction.

Which skills does a product designer need?

A product designer needs UX and UI craft, product discovery, problem framing, design systems, prototyping, product metrics, experimentation, AI product patterns and the confidence to explain design decisions to engineers and business teams.

Nasscom's Strategic Review 2026 describes AI moving from optional to core infrastructure in India's tech industry. When AI sits inside everyday products, someone must design how people use and trust it. That is why AI product design runs through years 2 and 3.

What will you build on the way?

You finish one project every month, and for a product designer the key pieces are discovery work, two design systems, AI prototypes and end-to-end product cases that show your thinking, not just your screens.

These are real projects from the Product & UX / AI Design roadmap, in the order a student meets them:

  1. Research-to-insight case study. Shows you can turn raw research into a product decision.
  2. Product discovery case study. Picking the right problem, the first job of a product designer.
  3. Mini design system. Your first set of reusable parts with clear rules.
  4. Production-style design system. A larger system, closer to what real product teams maintain.
  5. AI assistant UX prototype. Designing how people talk to and trust an AI feature.
  6. Enterprise product prototype. A complex tool with many users, roles and edge cases.
  7. End-to-end AI product case. One product designed from problem to tested solution.
  8. Flagship AI product design capstone. Your strongest portfolio piece, built over the final stage.

A parent can open the GitHub page at any time and see the work. That is often more convincing than a marksheet.

Which certificates help a future product designer?

For product designers, the NN/g UX Certification in year 3 is the most advanced one in this roadmap, but like every external certificate it is awarded by its provider, and a strong product portfolio still matters most.

The Product & UX / AI Design program prepares students for these 6. Each is awarded by its own certifying body after its own exam, and passing is not guaranteed.

  • IxDF UX Certification.
  • Google UX Design Professional Certificate.
  • CalArts UI/UX Design Specialization.
  • Google Project Management Professional Certificate.
  • Microsoft AI Product Manager Professional Certificate.
  • NN/g UX Certification. Nielsen Norman Group awards it after you pass 5 of its courses and their exams. It offers specialties including Artificial Intelligence, Interaction Design, Research and Management, and the certificate does not expire.

The roadmap also prepares for the IxDF, Google UX and CalArts certificates in year 1. Microsoft AI Product Manager in year 2 suits designers of AI products.

Who should not choose this path?

This path is not for you if you only want to make screens look beautiful, avoid numbers completely, or dislike debating ideas with a team, because product designers are judged on whether the product works.

  • You only want to polish visuals. Product design spends as much time on problems and flows as on looks. Visual or graphic design may suit you better.
  • Numbers make you uneasy. Metrics and experiments in years 2 and 3 decide whether a design succeeded.
  • You want to build the product in code. This program does not teach coding. The Software Development program does.
  • You expect a guaranteed job. No honest course can promise a job, salary or exam pass, and this one does not.
  • You want a quick course. Product design builds on UI and UX first. Plan for 4 hours a week for three years.

What should parents ask before choosing a course?

Parents should ask five things of any course: can I see the full curriculum, what will my child build, how is learning checked, who answers questions, and what exactly is promised.

  1. Can I see the whole curriculum, month by month? A good course shows every module and its hours before you pay.
  2. What will my child build, and where will I see it? Ask for project names and whether work goes on GitHub.
  3. How is learning checked, and how often? Regular exams and practicals show progress early, not only at the end.
  4. Who answers questions, and how quickly? Ask how doubts are handled between classes.
  5. What is promised, and what is not? Get the certificate, timings and fees in writing. Be careful of anyone who guarantees a job or salary.

Frequently asked questions

How many years does it take to become a product designer after 10th?

Plan for about three years of skill building, such as the 3-year, 624-hour Product & UX / AI Design program. Many people start as UI/UX designers and grow into product design with experience, and some employers also ask for a degree.

Can I become a product designer without maths?

Yes, for the design work itself. You do need comfort with basic numbers, because product designers read metrics and run experiments. A B.Tech through JEE Main needs Physics and Mathematics in Class 12.

What is the difference between a product designer and a UI/UX designer?

A UI/UX designer focuses on research, flows and screens. A product designer also helps decide what to build, keeps the product consistent through design systems, and measures results. Many product designers start in UI/UX roles.

What is AI product design?

It is designing products that use AI, such as assistants or smart suggestions. The designer decides how users ask, how the AI answers and how users can trust or correct it. The roadmap covers it from year 2.

Will I learn to code as a product designer?

No. The program covers GitHub for your portfolio but does not teach programming. Product designers work closely with engineers, so understanding how products are built helps, even without coding.
