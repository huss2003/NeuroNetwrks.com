In short

  • Yes, you can start after Class 10. No technical background is assumed. You begin with how computers and networks work.
  • This is a defence career. The daily work is logs, alerts, SIEM tools like Splunk and Microsoft Sentinel, and incident response, not breaking into systems.
  • Plan for about three years: foundations and a first SOC project in year 1, security operations and testing in year 2, then Splunk, Sentinel, threat hunting and SC-200 preparation in year 3.

A cyber security analyst protects an organisation by watching for attacks and responding when one happens. Most start in a security operations centre (SOC), reading alerts and logs, deciding what is real, and following a playbook to contain it. It is detective work done on screens, often as part of a shift team.

Can you start on the path to a cyber security analyst right after Class 10?

Yes. You can start preparing for a cyber security analyst career right after Class 10, beginning with computers, networks and logs, long before you sit in a real security operations centre.

Your Class 11 and 12 stream matters because it decides which degrees stay open. Analyst skills themselves come from practice, not from the stream.

Which skills does a cyber security analyst need?

A cyber security analyst needs networking and operating system basics, log analysis, SIEM tools such as Splunk and Microsoft Sentinel, query writing in KQL, incident response, threat hunting and the ability to write a clear incident report.

Nasscom's Strategic Review 2026 describes cybersecurity rising in importance as AI-powered threats grow. Defending against them is daily analyst work, which is why detection and threat hunting fill most of year 3.

What will you build on the way?

You build a project every month, so after three years you have 36 projects, including SOC dashboards, investigation reports and detection packs that show an employer how you think through an alert.

These are real projects from the Cybersecurity roadmap, in the order a student meets them:

  1. Mini SOC Dashboard + Brute-Force Investigation Report. Your first real investigation: spotting repeated login attempts in logs and writing up what you found.
  2. Year-1 SOC Simulation Project + Portfolio Review. A first taste of shift-style work, reviewed so you know what to improve.
  3. Enterprise Incident Response Exercise. Practises the steps a team follows when an attack is confirmed.
  4. Splunk SOC Investigation Portfolio. A set of Splunk investigations you can show in an interview.
  5. Microsoft Sentinel Detection Pack. Detection rules written in KQL for Microsoft's cloud SIEM.
  6. Threat Hunting Report + ATT&CK Coverage Matrix. Shows you can look for hidden attackers and map your coverage to MITRE ATT&CK.
  7. CySA+ Style Analyst Assessment. An analyst-style assessment that mirrors the kind of work CySA+ is about.
  8. SC-200 End-to-End Security Operations Lab. One lab covering triage, investigation and response in Microsoft's security tools.

A parent can open the GitHub page at any time and see the work. That is often more convincing than a marksheet.

Which certificates help a future cyber security analyst?

Analyst certificates help show an employer you understand security operations, and this roadmap prepares for several in order, from ISC² CC in year 1 to CySA+ and SC-200 in year 3, each awarded by its own body.

The Cybersecurity program prepares students for these 6. Each is awarded by its own certifying body after its own exam, and passing is not guaranteed.

  • ISC² Certified in Cybersecurity (CC). An entry-level ISC2 certification that needs no work experience. Its five domains include security operations and incident response.
  • Google Cybersecurity Professional Certificate.
  • CompTIA Security+.
  • CEH v13.
  • CompTIA CySA+. Validates the ability to detect, analyse and respond to threats. CompTIA lists about 4 years in a SOC or vulnerability analyst role as recommended experience, so treat it as a later goal.
  • Microsoft Security Operations Analyst Associate (SC-200). An intermediate exam assessing how you manage a security operations environment, respond to incidents and hunt threats, using tools such as Microsoft Sentinel, Defender XDR and KQL.

Timing notes for 2026: CompTIA launched CySA+ V4 (CS0-004) on 23 June 2026, and says the English V3 exam retires on 22 December 2026. Microsoft says the English SC-200 exam will be updated on 21 October 2026. The roadmap also prepares for the Google Cybersecurity Professional Certificate and CompTIA Security+.

Who should not choose this path?

This path is not for you if you want constant excitement, dislike reading logs for long stretches, or expect to be breaking into systems rather than protecting them.

  • You want action every minute. Much of SOC work is patient checking of alerts that turn out to be harmless. Spotting the one that is real takes focus.
  • You mainly want to hack. Analysts defend. The program teaches testing in year 2, but this career is about watching, investigating and responding.
  • You cannot keep sensitive information private. Analysts see logs, emails and data they must never share or misuse. Trust is part of the job.
  • You dislike writing. Every incident ends in a written case file. A clear report helps the whole team respond.
  • Shift work worries you. Some SOC teams monitor around the clock in shifts, so an early role can mean odd hours. Ask each employer.
  • You want a guarantee. No honest course can promise a job, salary or exam pass. This one does not, and it awards a NeuroNetworks AI Ed certificate, not a degree.

What should parents ask before choosing a course?

Parents should ask five things of any course: can I see the full curriculum, what will my child build, how is learning checked, who answers questions, and what exactly is promised.

  1. Can I see the whole curriculum, month by month? A good course shows every module and its hours before you pay.
  2. What will my child build, and where will I see it? Ask for project names and whether work goes on GitHub.
  3. How is learning checked, and how often? Regular exams and practicals show progress early, not only at the end.
  4. Who answers questions, and how quickly? Ask how doubts are handled between classes.
  5. What is promised, and what is not? Get the certificate, timings and fees in writing. Be careful of anyone who guarantees a job or salary.

Frequently asked questions

How many years does it take to become a cyber security analyst after 10th?

Plan for about three years of steady learning, such as the 3-year, 624-hour Cybersecurity program, which is designed around entry-level roles like SOC Analyst L1. Many employers also ask for a degree, so most students build these skills alongside Class 11, 12 and college.

Can I become a cyber security analyst without maths?

Yes. Analyst work depends on logic, careful reading and pattern spotting rather than advanced maths, and subnetting arithmetic is taught from the basics. A B.Tech through JEE Main does need Physics and Mathematics in Class 12.

What is a SOC, and is it where analysts start?

A security operations centre is the team that monitors an organisation's systems for attacks. Entry-level analysts often start there at L1, triaging alerts. The program's year 1 ends with a SOC simulation project.

Which is better for an analyst, Splunk or Microsoft Sentinel?

Both are SIEM tools, and employers use each. The roadmap teaches Splunk and then Microsoft Sentinel with KQL in year 3, so you can show work in both.

Does the program guarantee a job or a certification pass?

No. The program aims at job readiness through labs, monthly projects and a portfolio, but it does not guarantee a job, internship, salary or exam pass. Certificates are awarded by their own bodies.
