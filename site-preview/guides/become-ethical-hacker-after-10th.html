In short

  • Yes, you can start after Class 10, but not with hacking. You start with how computers, networks and Linux work, because you cannot test what you do not understand.
  • Hacking is practised only in authorised labs and with written permission. Testing any system without the owner's permission is illegal in India, even out of curiosity.
  • Plan for about three years: foundations and defence in year 1, ethical hacking and CEH v13 preparation in year 2, security operations and cloud in year 3.

An ethical hacker is hired to find weaknesses in an organisation's systems before criminals do. They test only with written permission, inside an agreed scope, and then explain each problem and how to fix it. The skill is the same as an attacker's. The permission and the purpose are what make it ethical.

Can you start on the path to an ethical hacker right after Class 10?

Yes. You can start learning ethical hacking right after Class 10, as long as you begin with networking, Linux and security basics, and practise attacks only in labs you are allowed to use.

The real decision now is your Class 11 and 12 stream, because it decides which degrees stay open. Ethical hacking skills come from practice in any stream.

Which skills does an ethical hacker need?

An ethical hacker needs networking, Linux and Windows knowledge, scripting, an understanding of how defenders work, web and network testing methods, clear report writing and, above all, the discipline to stay inside the permission given.

Nasscom's Strategic Review 2026 notes cybersecurity rising in importance as AI-powered threats grow. That is one reason organisations pay people to test their own systems, legally, before attackers do.

What will you build on the way?

You build a project every month in an authorised lab, so after three years you have 36 projects and a GitHub record that shows legal, documented hacking work rather than claims.

These are real projects from the Cybersecurity roadmap, in the order a student meets them:

  1. Cybersecurity concept map + lab notebook + GitHub repository setup. Starts the habit every ethical hacker needs: writing down each step you take in a lab.
  2. Office Network Diagram + Wireshark Packet Analysis Report. Teaches you to see a network the way a tester first sees it.
  3. Linux Hardening Checklist + Bash Security Toolkit. You learn to lock a system down before you learn to test one.
  4. Security Automation Toolkit v1 (Python + Bash + PowerShell). Your own small scripts, the first tools you understand line by line.
  5. Authorized Recon & Enumeration Assessment. Your first test with a defined scope, done the legal way.
  6. Red-Team/Blue-Team Privilege Escalation Lab. You attack and defend the same lab, so you see both sides of one weakness.
  7. Year-2 Ethical Hacking Capstone. A full authorised exercise that pulls together a year of hacking skills.

A parent can open the GitHub page at any time and see the work. That is often more convincing than a marksheet.

Which certificates help an future ethical hacker?

CEH v13 is the certificate most linked with ethical hacking, and the program prepares for it across year 2, but EC-Council awards it after its own exam, and passing is not guaranteed.

The Cybersecurity program prepares students for these 6. Each is awarded by its own certifying body after its own exam, and passing is not guaranteed.

  • ISC² Certified in Cybersecurity (CC).
  • Google Cybersecurity Professional Certificate.
  • CompTIA Security+.
  • CEH v13. EC-Council's knowledge exam is 4 hours with 125 multiple-choice questions. There are no formal prerequisites, but EC-Council recommends about 2 years of IT security experience or its official training.
  • CompTIA CySA+.
  • Microsoft Security Operations Analyst Associate (SC-200).

EC-Council describes CEH v13 as now including AI in its training, across 20 learning modules. An optional 6-hour practical exam on its cyber range leads to the CEH Master level. Before CEH, the roadmap also prepares for ISC² Certified in Cybersecurity (CC) in year 1 and CompTIA Security+ in year 2.

Who should not choose this path?

This path is not for you if you want to hack real websites or friends' accounts, want quick results, or cannot accept that most of the work is careful reading, note-taking and report writing.

  • You want to hack things you do not own. Testing without the owner's permission is illegal in India. This program teaches only in authorised labs, and it expects the same outside class.
  • You expect it to look like the films. Real ethical hacking is slow and methodical. Many hours of a test find nothing, and that is normal.
  • You do not want to learn the basics first. Year 1 is networks, Linux, Windows and defence. Skipping it makes year 2 impossible to follow.
  • Writing reports sounds boring to you. Every test ends in a written report. Clear writing matters as much as technical skill.
  • You want a guarantee. No honest course can promise a job, salary or CEH pass. This one does not, and it awards a NeuroNetworks AI Ed certificate, not a degree.
  • You only have a phone. Security labs need a laptop with plenty of memory to run virtual machines.

What should parents ask before choosing a course?

Parents should ask five things of any course: can I see the full curriculum, what will my child build, how is learning checked, who answers questions, and what exactly is promised.

  1. Can I see the whole curriculum, month by month? A good course shows every module and its hours before you pay.
  2. What will my child build, and where will I see it? Ask for project names and whether work goes on GitHub.
  3. How is learning checked, and how often? Regular exams and practicals show progress early, not only at the end.
  4. Who answers questions, and how quickly? Ask how doubts are handled between classes.
  5. What is promised, and what is not? Get the certificate, timings and fees in writing. Be careful of anyone who guarantees a job or salary.

Frequently asked questions

How many years does it take to become an ethical hacker after 10th?

Plan for about three years of steady learning, such as the 3-year, 624-hour Cybersecurity program, where ethical hacking comes in year 2 after a year of foundations. Many employers also look for a degree, so most students build these skills alongside Class 11, 12 and college.

Can I become an ethical hacker without maths?

Yes, for most day-to-day ethical hacking work, which relies on networking, operating systems and logic rather than advanced maths. Subnetting needs simple arithmetic, which is taught. A B.Tech through JEE Main does need Physics and Mathematics in Class 12.

Is ethical hacking legal in India?

Ethical hacking is legal when you have the owner's written permission and stay within the agreed scope. Accessing a computer without permission is covered by Section 43 of the IT Act, 2000, and Section 66 makes it a punishable offence when done dishonestly or fraudulently.

Where do students practise hacking in this program?

Only in authorised lab environments set up for the course. Students never test real websites, school networks or anyone's accounts, and the same rule applies to practice at home.

When does CEH v13 preparation happen?

In year 2. The second half of the year covers ethical hacking and CEH foundations, web application security, network penetration testing, Active Directory testing, and CEH domains, reporting and a capstone. EC-Council awards the certificate after its own exam.
