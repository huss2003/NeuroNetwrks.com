In short

  • An AI engineer owns the whole AI system, not just the model. That means the data, the model choice, the service around it, the tests and the monitoring.
  • Plan for about three years after Class 10. In this program, year 3 is where it comes together: RAG, agents, fine-tuning, AI safety and AI system design.
  • Keep maths and build software habits early. Maths helps you judge a model. Git, Linux and Docker help you ship it.

An AI engineer builds working products on top of AI. That can mean a chatbot that answers from a company's documents, an agent that completes tasks, or a model inside an app. The job joins software skills, machine learning and good judgement about what AI should and should not do.

What does an AI engineer do day to day?

An AI engineer spends the day making the AI part of a product work reliably: choosing or adapting a model, connecting it to data and software, testing its answers and watching it after release.

Typical tasks: deciding whether a ready-made model is enough or needs adapting, linking it to an organisation's data, and building the API that serves it. They write test sets, check answers for quality and safety, and track speed and running cost. They work closely with software engineers, product managers and data teams.

Main tools: Python, PyTorch, Git and GitHub, Linux, Docker, and cloud AI services on AWS and Azure. All of these appear in the AI/ML Engineering program's tool list.

How it differs from a machine learning engineer: a machine learning engineer mostly trains and maintains prediction models built from an organisation's own data. An AI engineer more often starts from large pretrained models and builds a full system around them, with retrieval, tools, safety checks and monitoring. The skills overlap a lot, and the same program covers both.

Can you start on the path to an AI engineer right after Class 10?

Yes, you can start after Class 10, and a future AI engineer should begin with strong Python and school maths, because building, testing and running AI systems later depends on both.

Which skills does an AI engineer need?

An AI engineer needs Python, data handling, machine learning, deep learning, LLM and RAG skills, agent design, cloud deployment, testing and evaluation of AI output, and a clear sense of AI safety and responsible use.

Nasscom's Strategic Review 2026 describes AI moving from an optional extra to core infrastructure in India's $315 billion tech industry. That shift is why this roadmap ends with deployed AI products rather than isolated models.

What will you build on the way?

You build a project every month, 36 in total, moving from Python exercises to a full AI product, so your GitHub shows a clear line from first model to deployed generative AI system.

These are real projects from the AI/ML Engineering roadmap, in the order a student meets them:

  1. AI productivity + evaluation project. Early practice in judging AI output, a habit every AI engineer needs.
  2. Year-1 production-style ML project. Your first model packaged like real software, not just a notebook.
  3. End-to-end AI service. Data, model and API working together as one service.
  4. LLM evaluation playground. Compares how language models answer, so you choose with evidence.
  5. Small-model adaptation project. Shows you can tune a smaller model for a specific task.
  6. AI platform architecture project. Plans the full system around models: data, serving and monitoring.
  7. End-to-end AI product. A finished product a user can try, the strongest proof of AI engineering.
  8. Final Professional AI/ML Capstone Project. Three years of skills brought into one portfolio piece.

A parent can open the GitHub page at any time and see the work. That is often more convincing than a marksheet.

What is a realistic first project for a future AI engineer?

A realistic first project is a small message sorter: a Python tool that labels short messages with a simple model, plus a test set that shows exactly where it fails.

In two or three weeks at home, build a tool that sorts short messages into a few groups. Try praise, complaint and question.

  • Week 1: write or collect about 100 short messages and label each one by hand. Train a simple text classifier with scikit-learn.
  • Week 2: turn it into a command-line tool that takes a new message and prints its label. Keep 20 tricky messages aside as a test set.
  • Week 3: log every wrong answer with a guess at why. Change one thing, such as adding examples, and compare the test results.

This is an AI system in miniature: data, model, interface and tests. In the program, the same idea grows into the End-to-end AI service project in year 2. There, data, model and API run as one service.

Where does prompt engineering fit in?

Prompt engineering, instructing AI models and testing their answers, is one skill inside AI engineer and AI developer roles; few jobs are only prompt writing, so it works best as a starting point.

A student can start early with AI Foundation, open from Class 9. Its Prompt Engineering & AI Evaluation module is 7 hours, followed by 8 hours on context engineering, embeddings and RAG.

For career-level depth, AI/ML Engineering covers generative AI, embeddings and RAG, agents and LLM evaluation in year 3.

Which certificates help an future AI engineer?

Vendor certificates such as Microsoft AI-901, AWS AIF-C01 and IBM AI Engineering show employers specific AI knowledge, but they support a portfolio rather than replace it, and each body awards its own credential after its own assessment.

The AI/ML Engineering program prepares students for these 5. Each is awarded by its own certifying body after its own exam, and passing is not guaranteed.

  • Microsoft Azure AI Fundamentals (AI-901). Microsoft lists the job role as AI Engineer. It is meant for people at the start of a career in AI solution development and expects some Python syntax.
  • AWS Certified AI Practitioner (AIF-C01). A foundational AWS exam on AI, ML and generative AI: 65 questions in 90 minutes, valid for 3 years.
  • Google AI Essentials.
  • NVIDIA DLI Fundamentals of Deep Learning.
  • IBM AI Engineering Professional Certificate. An intermediate 13-course series on Coursera covering PyTorch, Keras and TensorFlow, ending with a generative AI project using RAG and LangChain.

A 2026 note: Microsoft updated the English version of AI-901 on 15 April 2026. Study from the current guide on its exam page.

What laptop do you need to become an AI engineer?

You need a laptop or desktop with internet, because a phone alone is not enough for the labs. This is the setup recommended for the AI/ML Engineering program.

  • CPU: Intel i5 / Ryzen 5 or better
  • RAM: 16 GB minimum, 32 GB recommended
  • Storage: 512 GB SSD minimum, 1 TB recommended
  • GPU: Integrated GPU for basic ML; NVIDIA RTX 4060 8 GB+ recommended for the full roadmap
  • OS: Windows 11 + WSL2 or Linux

These are recommendations, not entry requirements. Ask us if your current computer is enough to start.

Who should not choose this path?

AI engineering is not for you if you want a guaranteed job, dislike maths and statistics, or expect AI tools to do the thinking for you instead of checking their work.

  • You want a guarantee. No honest course promises a job, salary or exam pass. This one does not.
  • Maths makes you switch off. Statistics and algebra come up every year. You do not need to be a topper, but you must stay with them.
  • You want to only use ChatGPT, not build with it. This path is about building and testing AI systems, not just writing prompts.
  • You cannot commit three steady years. Plan for 4 hours of classes a week, plus practice, for 36 months.
  • You only have a phone or a basic laptop. Training models needs a capable laptop. The recommended specification is shown on this page.
  • You need a degree and nothing else. This program gives a Neuronetworks AI Ed certificate, not a degree. Many students take it alongside school and college.

What should parents ask before choosing a course?

Parents should ask five things of any course: can I see the full curriculum, what will my child build, how is learning checked, who answers questions, and what exactly is promised.

  1. Can I see the whole curriculum, month by month? A good course shows every module and its hours before you pay.
  2. What will my child build, and where will I see it? Ask for project names and whether work goes on GitHub.
  3. How is learning checked, and how often? Regular exams and practicals show progress early, not only at the end.
  4. Who answers questions, and how quickly? Ask how doubts are handled between classes.
  5. What is promised, and what is not? Get the certificate, timings and fees in writing. Be careful of anyone who guarantees a job or salary.

Frequently asked questions

How many years does it take to become an AI engineer after 10th?

Plan for about three years of steady learning to build job-ready AI skills, such as the 3-year, 624-hour AI/ML Engineering program. Many employers also ask for a degree. The usual full route is Class 11 and 12, then a degree, with AI skills built alongside.

Can I become an AI engineer without maths?

Not really. You can use AI tools without maths, but building and evaluating models needs statistics, algebra and some calculus ideas. The program teaches maths for ML from school level, and keeping maths in Class 11 and 12 also keeps B.Tech through JEE Main open.

What is the difference between an AI engineer and a machine learning engineer?

The roles overlap. A machine learning engineer focuses on training, evaluating and running models. An AI engineer is broader and often builds whole products with generative AI, RAG and agents. This roadmap covers both, with year 3 leaning towards AI engineering.

Do I need coding experience before I start?

No. The program starts with computer and Python basics. Entry is from Class 11, with basic school-level maths and comfort using a computer.

Will this program get me a job as an AI engineer?

No program can promise that. This one builds skills, 36 projects and a GitHub portfolio aimed at roles such as Applied AI Engineer. It does not guarantee a job, internship or salary.
