In short

  • Yes, you can start after Class 10. No drawing talent or design background is assumed. Careful observation matters more.
  • Year 1 is fully about UI and UX: visual design, user research, information architecture, wireframes, interaction design and prototypes.
  • Your portfolio is your real qualification. Design employers look at case studies first, so every month ends with a piece of work you can show.

A UI/UX designer makes apps and websites easy and pleasant to use. The UX part means talking to users, finding where they get stuck and planning the flow. The UI part means the screens: layout, type, colour, buttons and how they respond when tapped.

Can you start on the path to a UI/UX designer right after Class 10?

Yes. You can start learning UI/UX design right after Class 10, because the program assumes no design background and begins with visual basics and how real people use the apps on their phones.

UI/UX design is open to every stream, which is unusual in tech. The stream mostly changes which degree you take alongside it.

Many UI/UX designers in India did not study design in school. What matters is a portfolio that shows how you think. Show the problem, the research, the options you tried and why you chose the final screen.

Which skills does a UI/UX designer need?

A UI/UX designer needs visual design, user research, information architecture, wireframing, interaction design, UI layout, prototyping, accessibility and the habit of testing designs with real users before calling them finished.

Nasscom's Strategic Review 2026 describes AI moving from optional to core infrastructure in India's tech industry. As more apps add AI features, someone has to design how people use them, which is why AI interfaces are part of this roadmap.

What will you build on the way?

You finish one design project every month, so year 1 alone gives you twelve pieces, from recreated app screens to a complete UX case study, and three years give you 36.

These are real projects from the Product & UX / AI Design roadmap, in the order a student meets them:

  1. Recreate 5 familiar app screens. Copying good screens trains your eye for spacing and layout.
  2. UX audit of a real product. Finding real problems in an app you already use.
  3. User-research study. Your first interviews and observations with real people.
  4. Mobile app wireframe set. The plan of an app before any visual polish.
  5. Interactive flow prototype. A clickable flow you can put in front of users.
  6. Web UI kit. A set of consistent buttons, forms and cards for a website.
  7. Complete Year-1 UX case study. Your first full portfolio piece, from research to final screens.
  8. Accessible AI interface. Proof that you design for everyone, including AI features.

A parent can open the GitHub page at any time and see the work. That is often more convincing than a marksheet.

Which certificates help a future UI/UX designer?

UI/UX certificates from Google, CalArts and IxDF can show an employer that you studied the basics properly, but each is awarded by its own provider, and design hiring still depends mostly on your portfolio.

The Product & UX / AI Design program prepares students for these 6. Each is awarded by its own certifying body after its own exam, and passing is not guaranteed.

  • IxDF UX Certification. IxDF courses are self-paced, with a certificate for each course you finish. IxDF says grading is done by experts, and its course list includes "User Experience: The Beginner's Guide".
  • Google UX Design Professional Certificate. A beginner-level series on Coursera covering UX research, information architecture, wireframing, prototyping, usability testing and responsive web design. Google says no degree or experience is required.
  • CalArts UI/UX Design Specialization. A 4-course series on Coursera from the California Institute of the Arts. It covers visual elements of UI design, UX fundamentals, information architecture and prototypes.
  • Google Project Management Professional Certificate.
  • Microsoft AI Product Manager Professional Certificate.
  • NN/g UX Certification.

These three sit in year 1, in the UX Foundations, UI Design and Year-1 Capstone modules. The NN/g UX Certification comes later, in year 3.

Who should not choose this path?

This path is not for you if you want to make art for its own sake, dislike feedback on your work, or prefer working alone, because UI/UX design means designing for other people and changing your ideas when users struggle.

  • You want to make art for yourself. UI/UX is about what users need, not personal style. Illustration or fine arts may suit you better.
  • Criticism of your work upsets you. Designs are reviewed and tested all the time. Changing them is normal.
  • You would rather not talk to strangers. User research means interviewing and watching real people.
  • You expect a guaranteed job. No honest course can promise a job, salary or exam pass, and this one does not.
  • You need a degree and nothing else. This program awards a NeuroNetworks AI Ed certificate, not a degree. Many students take it alongside school and college.

What should parents ask before choosing a course?

Parents should ask five things of any course: can I see the full curriculum, what will my child build, how is learning checked, who answers questions, and what exactly is promised.

  1. Can I see the whole curriculum, month by month? A good course shows every module and its hours before you pay.
  2. What will my child build, and where will I see it? Ask for project names and whether work goes on GitHub.
  3. How is learning checked, and how often? Regular exams and practicals show progress early, not only at the end.
  4. Who answers questions, and how quickly? Ask how doubts are handled between classes.
  5. What is promised, and what is not? Get the certificate, timings and fees in writing. Be careful of anyone who guarantees a job or salary.

What can a UI/UX designer earn in India?

Indicative ranges for product and UX roles in India are about ₹4 to 8 lakh a year for freshers, ₹10 to 22 lakh at 3 to 5 years, and ₹25 to 50 lakh or more at senior level.

Frequently asked questions

How many years does it take to become a UI/UX designer after 10th?

Core UI and UX skills take about a year in this roadmap. The full 3-year, 624-hour Product & UX / AI Design program builds a professional portfolio. Some employers also ask for a degree, so many students study it alongside Class 11, 12 and college.

Can I become a UI/UX designer without maths?

Yes. UI/UX design needs observation, empathy and clear thinking more than advanced maths. Mathematics matters only if you also want a B.Tech through JEE Main, which needs Physics and Mathematics in Class 12.

Do I need to be good at drawing to become a UI/UX designer?

No. Most UI work is layout, spacing and type, not drawing. The program starts with visual design fundamentals and assumes no design background.

What is the difference between UI and UX design?

UX design is how a product works for its users: the research, the flow and the problems it solves. UI design is how the screens look and respond. Most junior roles in India ask for both.

Does the program include AI in UI/UX design?

Yes, from year 2. Students study AI UX patterns and AI prototyping, then build an accessible AI interface and AI design systems in year 3.
