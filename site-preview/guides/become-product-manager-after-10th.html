In short

  • You can start building PM skills after Class 10, but product manager is rarely a first job. Most people move into it after experience.
  • The usual route runs through another role first: design, engineering or analysis, then a move into product management.
  • Design and research make a strong starting point. They teach you to understand users, which is the heart of product work.

A product manager decides what a product team builds next and why. They study users and the market, write requirements, set priorities and plan a roadmap. They keep designers, engineers and business teams moving in one direction. They usually do not design screens or write code themselves.

Can you start on the path to a product manager right after Class 10?

You can start learning product management skills after Class 10, but most product managers get the job title only after a few years in design, engineering or analysis, so treat this as a long-term plan.

Because PM usually comes later, your stream should keep open the role you will start in. Here is how each choice affects that first step.

Which skills does a product manager need?

A product manager needs user understanding, product discovery, clear requirements writing, prioritisation, agile planning, roadmaps, metrics, experimentation, AI product judgement and strong communication with designers, engineers and business teams.

Nasscom's Strategic Review 2026 describes AI moving from optional to core infrastructure in India's tech industry. That is why two year 2 modules prepare for the Microsoft AI Product Manager certificate.

What will you build on the way?

You build one project every month, and the ones that matter most for future product managers are the briefs, requirements, roadmaps, launch plans and strategy cases from years 2 and 3.

These are real projects from the Product & UX / AI Design roadmap, in the order a student meets them:

  1. Product brief + requirements. Your first written plan that a team could actually build from.
  2. Agile product simulation. Practice running work in sprints, as most product teams do.
  3. Product roadmap + KPI plan. What to build in which order, and how you will measure it.
  4. AI product requirements document. Requirements for a product that uses AI, including its limits.
  5. AI product launch plan. Planning how a product reaches users, not just how it is built.
  6. Experimentation case study. Using a test to decide, instead of opinion.
  7. AI product strategy portfolio. Shows long-term product thinking to a future employer.

A parent can open the GitHub page at any time and see the work. That is often more convincing than a marksheet.

Which certificates help a future product manager?

The Google Project Management and Microsoft AI Product Manager certificates in year 2 can show early product interest, but each is awarded by its provider, and they cannot replace the work experience most PM roles ask for.

The Product & UX / AI Design program prepares students for these 6. Each is awarded by its own certifying body after its own exam, and passing is not guaranteed.

  • IxDF UX Certification.
  • Google UX Design Professional Certificate.
  • CalArts UI/UX Design Specialization.
  • Google Project Management Professional Certificate. A 7-course beginner series on Coursera, including agile project management with Scrum. Note that it covers project management, which is about delivering work on time, not product management. Google says no experience or degree is required.
  • Microsoft AI Product Manager Professional Certificate. A 5-course beginner series on Coursera. Its listed skills include market research, competitive analysis, product roadmaps, product strategy, user stories and generative AI.
  • NN/g UX Certification.

Google also says its project management certificate counts towards education hours that help prepare for the Project Management Institute's CAPM certification.

Who should not choose this path?

This path is not for you if you want a product manager title straight after school, dislike making decisions others may disagree with, or want to spend your day making things with your own hands.

  • You want to be a PM straight after school. That is rare. Expect to start in design, engineering or analysis and move later.
  • You avoid hard decisions. Product managers say no to many good ideas and explain why.
  • You want to design or code all day. PMs mostly write, plan and talk. Look at UI/UX design or the Software Development program.
  • You expect a guaranteed job. No honest course can promise a job, salary or exam pass, and this one does not.
  • You dislike writing and meetings. Clear documents and calm discussions are most of the job.

What should parents ask before choosing a course?

Parents should ask five things of any course: can I see the full curriculum, what will my child build, how is learning checked, who answers questions, and what exactly is promised.

  1. Can I see the whole curriculum, month by month? A good course shows every module and its hours before you pay.
  2. What will my child build, and where will I see it? Ask for project names and whether work goes on GitHub.
  3. How is learning checked, and how often? Regular exams and practicals show progress early, not only at the end.
  4. Who answers questions, and how quickly? Ask how doubts are handled between classes.
  5. What is promised, and what is not? Get the certificate, timings and fees in writing. Be careful of anyone who guarantees a job or salary.

Frequently asked questions

How many years does it take to become a product manager after 10th?

Longer than most roles. Skills can be built in about three years, such as the 3-year, 624-hour Product & UX / AI Design program. But most people become product managers only after Class 12, a degree and a few years in design, engineering or analysis.

Can I become a product manager without maths?

Yes. Product managers need comfort with simple metrics, percentages and charts, not advanced maths. Keeping Mathematics does keep more first roles open, such as engineering, which needs Physics and Mathematics in Class 12 for JEE Main.

Can I get a product manager job right after college?

Some companies hire associate product managers from college, but these roles are few. Most product managers first work as designers, engineers or analysts, then move into product.

Is project management the same as product management?

No. Project management is about delivering agreed work on time. Product management is about deciding what should be built and why. Good product managers use both, which is why year 2 includes Google Project Management preparation.

Which first job leads best to product management?

There is no single route. Design and UX research teach you about users, engineering teaches how products are built, and analysis teaches you to read data. This program follows the design and UX route.
