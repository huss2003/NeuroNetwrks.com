**Class 10 Entry \| 3 Years \| 52 Weeks/Year \| 4 Hours/Week \| 624 Total Hours**

# 1. Program Purpose

This is a three-year, 624-hour Cybersecurity specialization designed for a student entering after Class 10 with no assumed technical background. The program is structured to move from absolute beginner knowledge to practical, professional-level cybersecurity capability. The objective is job readiness in entry-level cybersecurity roles through skills, hands-on laboratories, projects, GitHub evidence and a professional portfolio. No internship or employment placement is guaranteed by the program.

# 3. Exact Hour Model

The academic calendar uses four 5-week months per year (March, June, September and December) and eight 4-week months per year. Therefore each year contains 52 weeks and 208 hours, and three years contain exactly 156 weeks and 624 hours.

| **Year** | **Weeks** | **Hours** | **5-week months**                |
|----------|-----------|-----------|----------------------------------|
| Year 1   | 52        | 208       | March, June, September, December |
| Year 2   | 52        | 208       | March, June, September, December |
| Year 3   | 52        | 208       | March, June, September, December |
| Total    | 156       | 624       | 12 five-week months total        |

Each month ends with a one-hour internal examination. In a 4-week month, 15 hours are instruction/practical work + 1 hour examination. In a 5-week month, 19 hours are instruction/practical work + 1 hour examination.

# 4. Teaching & Assessment Model

- Theory: concepts are introduced from zero and connected to real cybersecurity work.

- Demonstration: instructor demonstrates the technique in a controlled lab.

- Hands-on lab: student performs the task independently in an authorized environment.

- Project: the student produces an artifact that can be documented in GitHub/portfolio.

- Examination: one hour after each major monthly topic; exams can contain theory, scenarios and practical tasks.

- Certification readiness: external certification objectives are embedded into relevant months rather than taught as isolated exam-cram blocks.

- Portfolio evidence: screenshots, sanitized logs, reports, scripts, diagrams, detection rules and write-ups are retained as evidence.

- Ethics and authorization: offensive-security work is performed only against intentionally vulnerable or explicitly authorized systems.
